# Estimating protein relaxation responses through quantum matrix functions

Project Pulsar has a literature-supported reason to investigate quantum response estimation: selected dynamical correlations can be computed through quantum transformations of a relaxation operator, and coherent estimation can improve the dependence of sampling work on precision. The practical opportunity depends on constructing that operator and the residue observables efficiently. The current implementation first calculates the complete reduced eigensystem classically, so its quantum circuit establishes an encoding and estimation benchmark rather than a faster route to those responses.

The next engineering question is explicit. Can we construct a circuit directly from the model’s force or contact factors, preserve the required response accuracy, and identify a useful cost regime against classical methods that receive the same factors? This creates a concrete connection between the existing measured representation, a bounded quantum experiment and a possible future computational benefit. Literature supports the investigation; a protein-specific implementation and matched cost measurements must establish the benefit.

## Direct correlation estimation provides the closest mathematical precedent

Kharazi and colleagues’ 2026 preprint is particularly relevant because it estimates matrix elements of a Fokker–Planck propagator, including dynamical correlations. Its construction combines unitary evolutions through a Gaussian integral and uses direct overlap estimation to avoid preparing a normalized, decayed output state. Theorem 8 assumes input-state oracles and a block encoding of a square-root factor, with its normalization and gate cost included. The comparison concerns worst-case analytical guarantees, rather than a lower bound excluding every faster classical method. Table I estimates 1.1 × 10⁸ Toffoli gates for a one-dimensional double-well example at additive error 0.01, excluding input preparation. This is an algorithmic precedent with substantial resource requirements, not a protein hardware result.[^1]

The correspondence to Pulsar follows from its own equations. Absorbing the fixed response normalization into the observable vectors, the reduced Gaussian calculation has the form

\[
C_{ij}(t)=B_i^{\mathsf T}\bigl(e^{-tH}-I\bigr)B_j,
\qquad H=H^{\mathsf T}\succeq0.
\]

The evolving term is precisely a bilinear function of a relaxation operator. Preparing the normalized vectors associated with two residue observables turns that term into an overlap, and the static inner product completes the response. This algebraic match is our interpretation of the literature, not a claim that the paper has validated this protein model.

Two distinctions matter. The Gaussian weight in the paper’s integration formula is a numerical construction; it is not validation of Pulsar’s Gaussian physical closure. Also, avoiding a decayed-state preparation penalty does not remove the need to resolve a small response to a biologically meaningful accuracy. An additive error guarantee can still be uninformative when the signal is smaller than the error.

Peer-reviewed work supports the broader connection. Danz and colleagues formulate frequency response estimation for classical coupled oscillators through quantum phase estimation, with explicit sparse-access, coefficient-precision and eigenvalue-resolution assumptions. Their response function and hard example differ from Pulsar’s overdamped contact-energy correlations, so their theorem is a mechanical-response precedent rather than a transferable speedup result.[^2] Babbush and colleagues likewise give quantum algorithms for selected properties of coupled classical oscillators under efficient spring, mass and initial-state access. Their computationally hard families do not establish that protein-derived Gaussian models have the same difficulty.[^3]

## Coherent estimation offers a precision benefit when oracle evaluations are expensive

Grinko and colleagues’ iterative amplitude estimation provides a rigorous ideal confidence-controlled estimator whose query cost scales approximately as the inverse target error, with logarithmic confidence factors. It avoids a quantum phase estimation register, but still uses repeated coherent applications of the preparation circuit and its inverse.[^4] This supports measuring an estimator’s precision curve; it does not make preparation or coherence inexpensive.

For an expectation generated by an executable stochastic calculation, Kothari and O’Donnell give a stronger variance-aware formulation. Their algorithm uses O(n) coherent calls to the generating code to reach error σ/n, where σ is its standard deviation. The ordinary sample mean reaches error proportional to σ/√n. The gate cost generally includes O(nS) when the generating circuit costs S gates.[^5]

For Pulsar, an expensive average across structure-derived model uncertainty could eventually fit that access model. However, a stored sample archive is not a reversible sampling circuit, and the current response calculation is a deterministic spectral sum. An ensemble extension would need a defined probability distribution, a reversible generator, a response observable and appropriate classical variance-reduction comparisons. It remains a secondary research direction until those components exist.

The most useful quantitative illustration is therefore conditional. At fixed confidence and ignoring constants, tightening a tolerance tenfold multiplies ordinary independent-sampling work by roughly 100 and ideal coherent mean-estimation work by roughly 10. This describes precision dependence. It does not predict a tenfold Pulsar runtime improvement because each coherent evaluation can be much more expensive and a deterministic classical alternative may already be available.

## The current spectral circuit already provides the data for a short classical sum

The inspected implementation computes an eigendecomposition H = VΛVᵀ and transformed observables U = VᵀB before constructing the circuit. The relaxation filter explicitly loads exp(−tλₐ). Once those arrays exist, the same response is

\[
C_{ij}(t)=\sum_{a=1}^{r}U_{ai}U_{aj}\operatorname{expm1}(-t\lambda_a).
\]

This is a length-r deterministic sum per entry. A complete matrix is Uᵀ diag(expm1(−tΛ)) U, allowing reuse of the spectrum and batched matrix multiplication. Counting a new diagonalization for every classical entry would misrepresent this comparator. Conversely, amortizing quantum compilation over repeated queries requires giving the classical implementation the same reuse.

The earlier rank 347 spectral study reports 13.73 ms for matched classical preprocessing and three response matrices once the earlier rank-347 KRAS operator exists, of which 0.808 ms is response evaluation. It also reports a 74.85-fold reduction in a calculated T-gate budget against a fixed sufficient sampling plan for the same quantum circuit. These have different denominators. The latter is an improvement within the modeled quantum estimator comparison and is not a speedup over the former.[^6]

The expanded operators reinforce the need to cost input access. Every entry of the saved reduced H matrices exceeds 10⁻¹² times the largest absolute entry in its matrix. That descriptive threshold shows these saved matrices are dense; it is not a tested sparsification or a proof that their physical factors lack exploitable structure.

These are address registers only, excluding flags, controls, arithmetic, reflections and hardware overhead. The expanded models require fresh quantum resource estimates; the older rank-347 costs cannot be attached to them. This audit also leaves the recorded KRAS numerical-qualification failure unchanged.[^7]

Zhang and Yuan show why this distinction matters: general data-access constructions can have nearly linear circuit cost in matrix dimension even for sparse matrices, whereas compact arithmetic or unitary structure can change the cost substantially.[^8] A small address register is consequently a representation fact, not a complete computational resource estimate.

## Direct access to physical factors is the architecture question that must be tested

Quantum singular value transformation supplies a general mechanism for applying polynomial functions to an operator embedded in a unitary circuit. The operator’s block encoding, normalization and approximation degree determine the work; they are inputs to the guarantee rather than automatically inexpensive components.[^9] Structured block-encoding constructions can exploit repeated values and efficiently described placement, but the best normalization and circuit cost depend on the actual matrix.[^10]

A possible construction can be stated without assuming its efficiency. If the model admits an accessible factor D satisfying H = D†D, define

\[
S=\begin{pmatrix}0&D^\dagger\\D&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
S^2=\operatorname{diag}(H,DD^\dagger).
\]

For t > 0, the exact identity

\[
e^{-tS^2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi t}}
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-s^2/(4t)}e^{-isS}\,ds
\]

places e⁻ᵗᴴ in the corresponding block. This is a mathematical route from unitary evolution to the desired overlap. Computing a dense square root or Cholesky factor first would merely move the loading problem. A useful implementation must expose an inexpensive description from the underlying model, compile it and retain its normalization in the estimate.

Pulsar’s Gaussian structure is relevant in both directions. Ornstein–Uhlenbeck spectral theory describes the semigroup through symmetric tensor sectors and sums of underlying mode eigenvalues.[^11] For the reversible Gaussian model, our degree-four contact observables therefore suggest a finite polynomial representation of size at most binomial(d + 4, 4) for d fluctuation coordinates, before exploiting additional symmetry. This degree-counting observation is an analytical inference. It provides a factorization candidate, but also supports classical Gaussian contractions and tensor methods. It does not create an exponential advantage merely by contrasting qubits with an explicit configuration grid.

A nonlinear drift generally changes the polynomial degree and can destroy exact finite closure. The original nonlinear-response discrepancy and earlier convergence failures therefore remain separate scientific problems. A future nonlinear operator must earn its own physical accuracy before a larger computational cost can justify quantum work. Reintroducing a previously unsuccessful basis without a new derivation and convergence evidence would not meet that requirement.

Tosta and colleagues provide a second algorithm candidate: randomized Chebyshev processing estimates bilinear matrix functions through modified Hadamard tests and can reduce average coherent depth relative to fully coherent constructions. Its state preparation, operator access, coefficient norms and statistical work still need to be counted.[^12] This is worth comparing on the same factor representation rather than choosing an algorithm solely from its headline asymptotic scaling.

## Classical algorithms must receive the same structure and return the same output

Al-Mohy and Higham’s matrix-exponential action algorithm computes eᵗᴬB without forming the full exponential or first diagonalizing A, with work dominated by matrix products. It accommodates blocks of right-hand sides and multiple times.[^13] Thus, avoiding diagonalization on the quantum side is a necessary architecture improvement, but not by itself a comparative advantage.

For the current Gaussian task, the benchmark should include cached spectral sums, batched CPU/GPU matrix multiplication and direct Gaussian moment contractions. For a new sparse or factorized operator, add matrix-free exponential action, Krylov methods and any justified tensor or low-rank approximation. Every implementation must use the same operator, response normalization, time, output set and admissible approximation error.

Cifuentes and colleagues’ matrix-function complexity classification reinforces this discipline: classical difficulty depends on the access model, normalization, sparsity, function and precision. Their catalog contains both hard and easy regimes. In particular, hardness of unitary time evolution is not a proof of hardness for this dissipative exponential.[^14] These results motivate checking the actual instance; they do not classify Pulsar as hard.

The output contract should distinguish three workloads. A single residue-pair response is one scalar. Screening candidate residues against a fixed functional site requires multiple entries or a separately derived aggregate estimator. Producing the complete N × N matrix requires explicitly recovering that many scalar outputs, with symmetry potentially reducing the count. A theorem for one overlap cannot silently become a cost claim for all three workloads.

The hybrid workflow retains complete matrices and nominations in the classical pipeline and uses the quantum experiment for predefined responses drawn from the same task. Both implementations evaluate the same declared metric. Full protein hardware execution has not been demonstrated; the quantum component supplies an actual response calculation and a testable development decision.

## A precision experiment can establish estimator value on the existing fixture

The existing local diagnostic is a three-site, rank-nine model with six active preparation qubits, 18 response queries and seven controls. Its native compilation gives 120–164 CZ gates and depths of 279–415 for the data circuits. The maximum ideal compiled response error against the reduced exponential is 1.77 × 10⁻⁷; the inherited discrepancy from the full Gaussian fixture is 2.10 × 10⁻⁵. These are local compilation and simulation results, not QPU measurements.[^15]

The current amplification wrapper requires four additional helper qubits for a six-qubit preparation. Its amplified construction therefore uses ten qubits. Routing, depth and gates must be compiled again; multiplying the six-qubit counts would omit the reflection implementation. The first proposed experiment should resolve that cost before requesting device execution.

Fix all 18 queries, including small responses, and use the same independent reference values. Compile the hardware-capped powers k ∈ {0, 1, 2, 4}, including preparation, inverse operations, reflections and routing. Compare direct quantum sampling, an ideal iterative amplitude-estimation reference that may choose larger powers under its original stopping rule, and a depth-capped estimator with an explicitly fitted noise model. The capped arm must establish its own coverage; it does not inherit the full adaptive algorithm’s guarantee. Report fitting time and any calibration data used.

The prospective response-tolerance curve should cover 0.05, 0.02, 0.01, 0.005 and 0.002. In the current circuit, Cᵢⱼ = (2pᵢⱼ − 1)Lᵢⱼ − Gᵢⱼ, where Lᵢⱼ = ‖Bᵢ‖‖Bⱼ‖ and Gᵢⱼ is the static inner product after response normalization. Probability error εₚ therefore contributes 2Lᵢⱼεₚ to response error. Allocate separate allowances for representation discrepancy, static-term arithmetic, circuit approximation, device bias and statistical uncertainty, using each model’s own norms.

For each tolerance, report worst-query error, simultaneous confidence coverage, total preparation calls, native two-qubit gates, maximum depth and complete elapsed execution cost. These are evaluation points, not promises of attainability. Simulated studies should use the actual circuit and declared noise model, followed by device measurements only if compilation and bias qualification support the intended budget.

Giurgica-Tiron and colleagues quantify the tradeoff between coherent depth and the total number of oracle calls; fixing the available depth limits the asymptotic precision saving.[^16] Tanaka and colleagues show estimation saturation under noise and report a three-qubit case without an advantage over their sampling comparator.[^17] These findings make depth and confidence part of the experiment, rather than quantities that can be omitted after counting shots.

The statistical claim must refer to the correct target. Confidence intervals around a noisy device probability do not automatically cover the ideal response of the declared Gaussian model. Use independent repetitions and a prespecified coverage analysis, allocate failure probability across the query family, and measure calibration drift. More shots reduce statistical uncertainty; they do not repair systematic bias or establish fidelity to the original nonlinear dynamics. A future block encoding must also propagate its additional normalization into the response error.

At the end of this experiment, a reduction against unamplified quantum sampling supports the wording “reduced quantum estimation work.” A practical computational gain requires a lower complete cost than the best classical implementation as well. The present fixture is primarily an engineering qualification case, because its deterministic reference is inexpensive.

## A factor implementation can determine whether a protein scale benefit is credible

The second proposed experiment should begin with one actual protein operator and signed residue observables. It should derive the factor representation before constructing the final dense reduced matrix, document equality or bounded discrepancy against the reference and count all arithmetic used to generate coefficients. The observable states must correspond to protein residues; uniform synthetic states would not qualify their preparation cost.

Use a hierarchy of submodels and then the four existing protein models, with physical and numerical accuracy held fixed. Record operator dimension, factor term count, block normalization, coefficient precision, input-state cost and compiled transformations. Where compilation exceeds a declared cap, report an analytic estimate with the uncompiled portion identified rather than treating it as measured execution.

Select test outputs before inspecting quantum performance. A suitable first workload is a fixed set of sender-to-functional-site responses at previously defined times. A later workload may estimate a contrast between two perturbations or a time-integrated response, provided its biological interpretation and the corresponding reference are defined. A contrast is not automatically cheaper to estimate; cancellation and state normalization must remain in its error budget.

The continuation decision should use complete costs. Let P denote setup time in seconds, U the number of scientifically distinct query batches that reuse the model, J the outputs per batch, qⱼ the number of quantum oracle calls for output j and gⱼ the elapsed seconds per call under a declared execution schedule. Repeating an identical query must allow cached answers on both platforms; it cannot create artificial amortization. For equal-work batches, a useful accounting identity is

\[
T_Q(U)=P_Q+U\left[\sum_{j=1}^{J}q_jg_j+T_{\mathrm{readout}}+T_{\mathrm{fit}}\right],
\]

compared with T_C(U) = P_C + U E_C for the fastest validated classical implementation, where E_C is seconds per batch. The expression is an accounting framework, not a scaling theorem; unequal batches require summing their measured work separately. It must include state preparation, inverse circuits, coefficient synthesis, routing, device reset, mitigation or error correction, data transfer and output recovery where relevant. Device queue time should be reported separately because it is variable and can obscure computational timing. Monetary charges and primitive gate counts require separate ledgers rather than substitution into this time equation.

Report both first-use and repeated-use comparisons, and separate money from elapsed time. A crossover is credible only if it survives plausible changes in preparation cost, normalization, physical error and hardware throughput. Fault-tolerance resource studies show that overhead can overwhelm a quadratic query advantage under realistic architectural assumptions; this is a reason to calculate constants, not a universal impossibility result.[^18]

The quantum branch should advance toward a practical-benefit claim only when three conditions hold together: its representation meets the scientific error requirement, its complete cost model is validated by compiled instances, and a reachable regime improves time or cost against the strongest classical alternative. If the Gaussian factors remain cheaper classically throughout the relevant range, retain them for the deliverable and record that outcome. A different nonlinear or ensemble branch then needs a separately justified scientific task and oracle, rather than an extrapolated Gaussian speedup.
