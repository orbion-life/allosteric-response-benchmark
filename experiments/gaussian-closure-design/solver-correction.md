# Gaussian algebra solver: numerical portability correction

12 September 2026. The initial Linux CI run stopped at the unchanged stationarity test. The original Newton line search compared two separately rounded objective values. Near the optimum, their difference is smaller than floating-point resolution, so a valid full Newton step can be rejected repeatedly. The original script and its original local result remain in `history/initial-algebra_check.py` and `history/initial-algebra-check.json`.

The revised helper evaluates the same objective difference without subtracting nearly equal totals. For a symmetric step D, let x be the eigenvalues of L⁻¹DL⁻ᵀ, where C=LLᵀ. The difference equals the inner product of the original gradient with D, plus Σ[x−log(1+x)]/(2β), plus the quartic quadratic increment in D. A short convergent series evaluates x−log(1+x) near zero. The line search compares this value directly with its original Armijo bound. It does not add an objective-increase allowance or change the optimum, gradient tolerance (10⁻¹¹), iteration limit (100), objective, physical model or frozen experiment protocol.

Five regression tests check a decrease smaller than one floating-point spacing against a 70-digit decimal calculation; agreement with direct objective differences away from cancellation; rejection of indefinite trials; thirty equivalent rotated-coordinate/multiple-start solves; and near-stationary harmonic starts. The historical solver stalled in six of the thirty fixed local cases. The corrected solver passed all thirty at the original tolerance. These counts describe the recorded local environment, not every platform. `solver-correction.json` records hashes and numerical differences. Linux CI must be rerun before claiming the cross-platform failure is resolved.
